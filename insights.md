# Key Insights — Banking Customer & Transaction Analysis

All figures below were generated by running the queries in
[`sql/02_analysis_queries.sql`](../sql/02_analysis_queries.sql) against
`data/banking.db`. They are real outputs from the dataset, not illustrative
placeholders — use them as talking points in interviews.

## Portfolio Snapshot
- **10,000 customers**, **10,000 transaction/account records**, **1,000
  branches**, spread across **6 cities** (Mumbai, Pune, Nashik, Nagpur,
  Kolhapur, Solapur) and **4 regions** (North, South, East, West).
- Customers are split fairly evenly across account types: **Current
  (33.6%)**, **Savings (33.5%)**, **Business (32.9%)**.

## Customer Segmentation
- Age distribution skews toward established, older customers:
  36–50 (2,322), 51–65 (2,258), 65+ (2,212), 25–35 (1,688), 18–24 (1,020).
  Nearly 65% of customers are over 36 — marketing for retirement/
  investment products likely outperforms youth-focused campaigns.
- **High-value segmentation** (combined balance across accounts):
  - High Value (≥ ₹1,00,000): **1,619 customers (25.6%)**
  - Mid Value (₹50,000–₹99,999): **2,636 customers**
  - Standard (< ₹50,000): remainder
  - Top customer: `Customer_ID 203801`, combined balance **₹4,10,103**.

## Engagement & Retention
- Customers average **1.58 transaction records each** (10,000 records
  across 6,335 unique customers) — engagement is thin, which limits
  true multi-visit retention analysis (see Limitations below).
- **3,412 customers (54% of active customers)** had no recorded
  transaction in the trailing 12 months relative to the latest date in
  the data (2025-03-20) — a large dormant pool worth prioritizing for
  win-back campaigns.
- Cohort retention (customers still transacting in the most recent year
  vs. their first-transaction year) is low — 7–8% for 2022–2024 cohorts —
  reinforcing that this dataset behaves more like periodic account
  snapshots than a continuous behavioral log.

## Transaction & Growth Trends
- Total transaction value grew **+27.7% in 2023 vs 2022**, then growth
  slowed to **+6.4% in 2024**; 2025 shows a decline but only reflects
  Jan–Mar (partial year).
- Monthly transaction counts are fairly stable (~230–280/month) with no
  strong seasonality spike, suggesting steady, non-seasonal banking
  activity in this population.

## Regional & City Performance
- **Top 5 cities by total balance:** Kolhapur (₹9.07 Cr) > Mumbai
  (₹8.83 Cr) > Nashik (₹8.60 Cr) > Pune (₹8.41 Cr) > Solapur (₹8.00 Cr).
- At the **region** level, East leads on total deposits (₹13.7 Cr across
  264 branches / 1,683 customers), but **average branch profit margin is
  low across all four regions (21.7%–25.6%)** — deposit volume is not
  translating into strong average profitability anywhere.

## Branch Profitability
- Profit margins are highly polarized: **6 branches operate above 99%
  margin** while **474 of 1,000 branches (47.4%) sit below the 20%
  "underperforming" threshold**.
- This bimodal spread (a few outlier high performers dragging up
  regional averages while nearly half the network underperforms) is a
  strong candidate for a follow-up root-cause analysis — e.g. comparing
  branch expense structures rather than revenue alone.

## Investment Product Preferences
- Investment amounts are broadly similar across Fixed Deposit, Recurring
  Deposit, and Mutual Fund products for all three customer types
  (Employee, Business, Individual) — no single product dominates,
  suggesting an opportunity for more differentiated, type-specific
  investment offers rather than a one-size-fits-all pitch.

## Data Limitations (worth stating proactively in an interview)
1. **Sparse per-customer history** — most customers have only 1–2
   transaction rows, so "dormancy" and "retention" here approximate
   engagement recency rather than tracking a full account timeline.
2. **Missing values** — `Age`, `Customer_Type`, and `City` are missing
   for ~500 customers each; `Firm_Revenue` is missing for 50 branches.
   Queries explicitly handle these via `WHERE ... IS NOT NULL` filters
   or a `CASE ... ELSE 'Unknown'` bucket rather than silently dropping
   rows.
3. **Single bank entity** — all records belong to one bank (HDFC Bank in
   this dataset), so comparisons are branch/region-level, not
   cross-bank.
